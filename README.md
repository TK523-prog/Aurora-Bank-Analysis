# Aurora-Bank-Analysis
This is a project aiming at drawing insights from Aurora Bank dataset. The goal of this project is to answer the question "How can Aurora Bank develop a multi-dimensional risk categorization framework to improve borrower segmentation and institutional stability?"

Drawing from the lessons of the 2008 financial crisis, which highlighted the dangers of ignoring the basics of lending and relying on single metrics like credit scores to evaluate customer financial health. We have developed a multi-dimensional risk categorization framework for Aurora Bank with the aim of improving customer segmentation and institutional stability. The framework evaluates three core indicators: Debt-to-Income (DTI) ratio, Credit Utilization, and Credit Score, where customers are flagged when they cross the threshold for those indicators and categorized into 4 segments (Low Risk, Moderate Risk, Elevated Risk, High Risk) depending on the number of thresholds they cross. 
We identified that about 22% of Aurora’s portfolio falls into the Low-Risk segment where these customers maintain a massive financial safety net and are predominantly aged 65 or older. We believe that Aurora would benefit from this segment by focusing on merchant interchange fee revenue through rewards for everyday essentials purchases and cross-sell wealth management services. 
Moderate-Risk segment represents the largest portion of the base customer at 49.05%. This group has a “Fair” to “Good” median credit score but has a relatively high Debt-to-Income ratio compared to the previous segment, which suggests that they are heavily reliant on credit to manage cash flow. We suggest Aurora Bank offers lower interest debt consolidation loans to capture interest profit from external competitors and introduce small credit lines to prevent failed transaction friction for this segment. 
For the Elevated-Risk segment (27.5%), while their median credit score is in the “Good” range, their median DTI of 51% is a major red flag and could be easily overlooked if Aurora only relies on Credit Score. This DTI indicates that they spend over half of their gross income on debt, so we suggest implementing a hard cap on unsecured credit increases for those in this segment with DTIs over 50%. 
High-Risk segment accounted for only 1.4% of the total customer base with 28 customers and only two active customers. This segment has an extremely shallow pool to draw out any reliable universal trend. We suggest immediately freezing available credit lines and conducting manual reviews of these two remaining active accounts to check for signs of fraud.
